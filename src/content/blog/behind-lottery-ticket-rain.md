The album's emotional peak: an actual lottery ticket, found folded in a coat with a receipt, scratched together over an empty plate. The numbers don't match. They never match. And the song decides — mid-chorus — that the scratching was the prize.

## The scene it came from

Small change emptied by the sink, a TV lighting an empty plate, shoes beside the door. One person says the album's smartest line like it's nothing: luck is just a name for the things we want. The verse before the last chorus changes exactly one word from its first version — listen for it.

## Sound notes

128 BPM with the biggest drop on the record. The scratch sound itself — coin on silver foil — is pitched and tuned into the lead percussion. On the final chorus everything mutes for half a bar right where the winning number should land, and the bounce crashes back in on the miss.

## One line

> 数字が 当たらなくても この遊びが 好きだ — even if the numbers never match, I love this game we play

Losing the lottery and winning the evening. That's the whole record in one scratch.
