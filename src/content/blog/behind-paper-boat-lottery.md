The title track's thesis in miniature: paper plus luck. You fold a wish into a receipt, set it on a puddle, and let the water decide. It's the album's gentlest track — a gamble with no money, no numbers, no way to lose that matters.

## The scene it came from

A street corner after rain. Everything in the song is small on purpose: one leaf for a sail, gum under a rail, a name swept away by water. The boat is tiny and brave, and the narrator quietly asks it to share what it knows. It's a wishing song wearing a club track's clothes.

## Sound notes

128 BPM stripped back to almost nothing at the start — just fingersnaps and water sounds recorded in a sink — before the full bounce floods in on the second chorus like the puddle overflowing. The last chant slows the chops down for the only time on the album.

## One line

> 浮いたら たぶん 私のだ — if it floats, it's probably mine

The loosest possible claim on luck. Not "it's mine" — *probably* mine. That hedge is the whole personality of the record.
